# Coverage, Risk & Financial Tools

This cluster covers everything in C2C that touches money and risk at the property level: insurance policy tracking and coverage analysis, risk-driven premium optimization, a governed insurance-quote handoff (not a live rate API), the "Hidden Savings & Benefits" program finder, ownership-cost/budget/financing modeling, a home reserve fund, property appreciation tracking, property tax review and appeal, purchase/repair negotiation coaching, contractor quote comparison, and homeowner-side insurance claims tracking. It's largely a Job-2 ("help me make the right home decision") cluster — most of these are decision-support tools a homeowner opens when facing a specific choice, not daily-attention surfaces. A recurring pattern across this cluster is a shared, versioned **financial assumptions / property-context envelope** (`services/financialContext/context.ts`, `services/financialAssumption.service.ts`, `AssumptionSet`) that most of these tools resolve against, so numbers stay consistent, reproducible, and traceable across tools instead of each one guessing independently — a quieter but real piece of the platform's "what is this based on?" trust story. Most of the modeling here is deterministic (rules, curated reference data, or user-entered facts) rather than AI-generated — Gemini is used narrowly (budget recommendations, appreciation insights, tax-bill OCR) and is called out explicitly below where it applies.

### Coverage Intelligence, Comparison & Insurance Handoff
- **What it does:** Analyzes a homeowner's current insurance + warranty posture against configurable risk-tolerance assumptions (premium, deductible, warranty cost, cash buffer) to recommend whether to keep, change, or shop coverage; lets homeowners build a side-by-side comparison of quote documents or policy terms; and — when they want new quotes — routes them through a **governed handoff** to a vetted recipient rather than calling a live insurance-rate API.
- **User flow:** 1) Homeowner opens the Coverage Intelligence tool for a property. 2) Reviews current coverage review questions and policy-record readiness. 3) Runs coverage analysis (optionally simulating override scenarios without saving). 4) Optionally opens a coverage comparison workspace, adds quote-document or policy-term options with confirmed/pending facts. 5) Records a decision (KEEP / CHANGE / SHOP / DEFER / PROFESSIONAL_REVIEW). 6) If shopping, consents to an insurance handoff request to an approved recipient; when the recipient returns a quote, its facts are ingested back into the comparison.
- **Frontend:** `/dashboard/coverage-intelligence` (**correction:** redirects to `/dashboard/protect?tab=coverage` per `next.config.js`'s "Protect consolidation," not directly to the property-scoped tool as previously stated — the property-scoped tool below is real and still the actual implementation, just not what this specific top-level URL leads to anymore) → `apps/frontend/src/app/(dashboard)/dashboard/properties/[id]/tools/coverage-intelligence/CoverageIntelligenceToolClient.tsx` (composes `PolicyRecordReadinessPanel`, `CoverageReviewQuestionsPanel`, `RiskPremiumOptimizerPanel`, and the legacy insurance-trend/renewal view). Comparison/decision UI: `.../tools/coverage-options/CoverageOptionsClient.tsx`. Components: `components/coverage/CoverageComparisonPanel.tsx`, `InsuranceHandoffPanel.tsx`, `InsuranceMarketContextPanel.tsx`, `PolicyRecordReadinessPanel.tsx`, `CoverageReviewQuestionsPanel.tsx`.
- **Backend:** `coverageAnalysis.routes.ts` → `getCoverageAnalysis`/`runCoverageAnalysis`/`simulateCoverageAnalysis`, `getCoverageComparisonWorkspace`/`addCoverageOption`/`recordCoverageComparisonDecision`, `getCoverageReview`, `getInsuranceHistory` (`controllers/coverageAnalysis.controller.ts`, `services/coverageAnalysis.service.ts`, `coverageComparison.service.ts`, `coverageReview.service.ts`). Handoff: `insuranceQuote.routes.ts` → `POST/GET .../insurance-handoff/requests`, `.../readiness`, `.../withdraw`, `.../returned-quote` (`services/insuranceHandoff.service.ts`). Market benchmarks: `insuranceMarketContext.routes.ts` (`services/insuranceMarketContext.service.ts`).

### Risk Premium Optimizer
- **What it does:** Models how specific home-hardening actions (e.g., mitigations) could plausibly reduce insurance premium exposure, and tracks a mitigation plan (recommended → planned → completed) tied to evidence documents or logged home events.
- **User flow:** 1) Homeowner opens the optimizer (embedded in Coverage Intelligence and available standalone). 2) Runs the optimizer with optional overrides (premium, deductible, cash buffer, risk tolerance, assumed new mitigations). 3) Reviews the resulting plan items and updates their status as mitigations are completed, attaching evidence.
- **Frontend:** `/dashboard/properties/[id]/tools/risk-premium-optimizer/RiskPremiumOptimizerToolClient.tsx`; also embedded as `components/ai/RiskPremiumOptimizerPanel.tsx` inside Coverage Intelligence.
- **Backend:** `riskPremiumOptimizer.routes.ts` → `GET .../risk-premium-optimizer`, `POST .../run`, `PATCH .../plan-items/:id` (`controllers/riskPremiumOptimizer.controller.ts`, `services/riskPremiumOptimizer.service.ts`).
- **Data:** Risk-premium optimization run + plan-item tables (see `riskPremiumOptimizer.service.ts`); resolves through the shared `AssumptionSet`/financial-context mechanism.
- **Notes:** Despite the "AI" panel naming in the frontend, the service itself is deterministic assumption-based modeling (no `GoogleGenAI` calls found in `riskPremiumOptimizer.service.ts`) — it's a calculator, not an LLM.

### Hidden Savings & Benefits
- **What it does:** Surfaces tax exemptions, rebates, utility incentives, insurance discounts, and similar programs a property may qualify for ("benefits"), and separately compares recurring household costs like utility/insurance plans against alternatives ("recurring cost" opportunities) — unified into one workspace with tabs for Benefits, Recurring, In Progress, and Realized.
- **User flow:** 1) Homeowner opens Savings & Benefits for a property. 2) Backend runs a detection scan evaluating curated, reviewed programs against the property's attributes (state/zip/region, dwelling facts) via a rule engine. 3) Homeowner views matches by confidence/category, views program detail, and can mark a match Viewed, Dismissed, or **Pursuing**. 4) For programs needing sensitive eligibility facts (income, disability, veteran status, etc.), the homeowner is asked and can consent, decline, or have the fact hard-deleted later. 5) Pursuing a benefit or recurring-cost switch creates a canonical **action** (save/dismiss/prepare/open official source/request quote/consent to partner handoff/mark externally submitted/switched/schedule follow-up). 6) The action's real-world result is recorded as an **outcome** trail (SUBMITTED → APPROVED/DENIED/WITHDRAWN → RECEIVED), which is the only thing that can post a verified savings/benefit realization — separate from the "pursuing" display status.
- **Frontend:** `/dashboard/properties/[id]/tools/savings-benefits/SavingsBenefitsClient.tsx` (tabs: benefits / recurring / in-progress / realized; composes `HiddenAssetFinderClient.tsx`, `HomeSavingsCheckPanel`, `SavingsBenefitsUnifiedPanels.tsx`). Both `/dashboard/properties/[id]/tools/hidden-asset-finder` and `/dashboard/properties/[id]/tools/home-savings` now redirect into this unified workspace (`section=recurring` for the latter) rather than being separate destinations.
- **Backend:** Benefits: `hiddenAssets.routes.ts` (`GET .../hidden-assets`, `POST .../refresh`, `GET .../coverage`, `PATCH .../property-hidden-asset-matches/:id`, outcome trail endpoints) + `hiddenAssetSensitiveFacts.routes.ts` (consent-gated facts, never returns a captured value, hard-deletes on request). Recurring cost: `homeSavings.routes.ts` (categories, summary, category detail, account upsert, run comparison, opportunity status/outcome). Unified read + actions: `savingsBenefitsUnified.routes.ts`. Admin/editorial CRUD + DRAFT→SUBMIT_FOR_REVIEW→APPROVE→PUBLISH lifecycle for sources and programs: `savingsBenefitsAdmin.routes.ts` (capabilities `SAVINGS_BENEFITS_AUTHOR`/`REVIEW`/`PUBLISH`). Rule evaluation lives in `services/hiddenAssets/ruleEngine.ts` (`evaluateProgram`, `buildPropertyAttributeMap`).
- **Data:** `HiddenAssetSource`, `HiddenAssetProgram` (+ `HiddenAssetProgramVersionSnapshot`, `HiddenAssetProgramRule`), `PropertyHiddenAssetMatch`, `PropertyHiddenAssetCriterionResult`, `HiddenAssetMatchOutcome`, `PropertyHiddenAssetSensitiveFact`, `PropertyHiddenAssetScanRun`, `SavingsBenefitAction`, `SavingsBenefitPartner`, `SavingsBenefitPartnerComplaint`, `HomeSavingsRun`, `HomeSavingsOpportunity`, `HomeSavingsOpportunityOutcome`, `HomeSavingsAccount`, `HomeSavingsCategory`.
- **Notes:** **Confirmed:** the historical `CLAIMED` status has been fully renamed to **`PURSUING`** in this feature — the only `CLAIMED` hits left in the codebase are in an unrelated job-queue lease mechanism (`ask/askOrchestrator.service.ts`) and an unrelated warranty-rights status enum, not this feature. Program/eligibility matching is a real deterministic rule engine against curated, source-reviewed program data (editorial workflow with sources, not AI-generated or scraped live). Sensitive facts follow a real minimization pattern: `GET` never returns the captured value, and `DELETE` is a genuine hard-delete. Separately, the `(dashboard)/savings` App Router route group (as opposed to `(dashboard)/dashboard/savings-benefits`) contains only a `.gitkeep` file — it is an empty placeholder, not a live alternate entry point; the real top-level link is `/dashboard/savings-benefits`, which redirects into this same property-scoped tool.

### Ownership Cost Intelligence
- **What it does:** Gives homeowners a versioned, forecastable view of their true annual cost of ownership (taxes, insurance, maintenance, utilities, etc.) with scenario planning, cost-change explanations, and cost-variability/planning decisions.
- **User flow:** 1) Homeowner opens Ownership Costs for a property. 2) Views the current snapshot, drivers of recent changes, and a multi-year forecast. 3) Creates "what-if" scenarios with overrides and compares them against the canonical forecast. 4) Reviews cost variability and records a planning decision (e.g., set aside a monthly buffer). 5) Buffer decisions can hand off directly into the Budget Forecaster (`ownershipCostDecisionId` / `ownershipCostMonthlyBufferCents` query params).
- **Frontend:** `/dashboard/ownership-costs` (resolver → primary property) → `/dashboard/properties/[id]/tools/ownership-costs`. - **Backend:** `ownershipCosts.routes.ts` → `GET .../ownership-costs`, `.../decisions`, `.../variability`, `.../forecast`, `.../changes`, scenario CRUD (`.../scenarios`), notification preferences; legacy `tools/true-cost` etc. now return `410 OWNERSHIP_COST_LEGACY_API_RETIRED` with a `canonicalUrl`. Admin/ops: `ownershipCostOperations.routes.ts` (`dashboard`, `replay` for reproducibility auditing, `launch-gate`). Core logic in `services/ownershipCosts/ownershipCostForecast.service.ts`, `ownershipCostVariability.service.ts`, `ownershipCostChange.service.ts`, `ownershipCostConsumerProjection.service.ts`.
- **Data:** `OwnershipCostDefinition`, `OwnershipCostAdapterRun`, `OwnershipCostObservation`, `OwnershipCostSnapshot` (+`SnapshotLine`), `OwnershipCostScenario`, `OwnershipCostForecast` (+`ForecastLine`), `OwnershipCostChange`, `OwnershipCostDecision`.
- **Notes:** Every forecast/snapshot is versioned (`definitionVersion`, `methodVersion`, `calculationFingerprint`) specifically so other tools (Break-Even, Budget Forecaster) can cite it as a reproducible source rather than recomputing their own numbers — this is the backbone the rest of the financial cluster leans on. ### Break-Even Calculator
- **What it does:** Projects, year by year, when cumulative home-equity/appreciation gains are projected to overtake cumulative ownership costs — i.e., when owning "breaks even" versus the money spent — with a debt-aware mode if mortgage details are on file.
- **User flow:** 1) Homeowner opens the tool for a property, choosing a 5- or 10-year horizon. 2) Reviews the year-by-year projection, break-even point (or "not reached"), and a conservative/base/optimistic sensitivity range. 3) Can override individual assumptions (appreciation, growth rates, mortgage terms) via query params, which get persisted into a reusable `AssumptionSet`. 4) Gets routed to a relevant next tool (Sell/Hold/Rent, or Capital Timeline) based on the result.
- **Frontend:** `/dashboard/properties/[id]/tools/break-even/BreakEvenClient.tsx` (+`breakEvenApi.ts`).
- **Backend:** `breakEven.routes.ts` → `GET /api/properties/:propertyId/tools/break-even` (`controllers/breakEven.controller.ts` → `services/breakEven.service.ts`).
- **Data:** No dedicated persistence model for the result itself — it's computed live from `Property`, `PropertyFinancingProfile`, the Ownership Cost Intelligence snapshot/forecast, and a resolved `AssumptionSet`.
- **Notes:** Appreciation rate is a genuine external data integration where possible: `AppreciationIndexService` pulls **FHFA repeat-sale House Price Index** comps by region, and only falls back to a labeled 3.5% national heuristic when FHFA data doesn't resolve for the property's area — the response explicitly flags which source (`FHFA` vs `HEURISTIC`) was used, with a confidence level and fallback chain. This is one of the more rigorously source-labeled tools in the cluster.

- **User flow:** 1) Homeowner opens Budget from the dashboard and selects a property. 2) Views the monthly forecast, category breakdown, and confidence level (which rises with more inventory data on file). 3) Reads AI-generated recommendations tailored to their total spend and property age.

### Home Reserve Fund
- **What it does:** Tracks a dedicated savings "reserve fund" for known future capital expenses (e.g., roof, HVAC replacement), including funded line items, contributions, and automatic reconciliation suggestions against observed bank/expense activity.
- **User flow:** 1) Homeowner opens Reserve Fund for a property. 2) Sees the fund balance and target, plus line items tied to specific future capital needs. 3) Logs contributions manually, or reviews live-computed reconciliation suggestions and accepts a match to auto-log a contribution. 4) Retires a line item once its expense has occurred. 5) Can trigger a recalculation of the fund's target/status.
- **Frontend:** `/dashboard/properties/[id]/tools/reserve-fund/ReserveFundClient.tsx` (+`reserveFundApi.ts`).
- **Backend:** `homeReserveFund.routes.ts` → `GET/PATCH .../reserve-fund`, `POST .../recalculate`, line items (`GET`, `POST .../retire`), contributions (`GET`, `POST`, `DELETE`), reconciliation suggestions (`GET`, `POST .../accept`) (`controllers/homeReserveFund.controller.ts`).
- **Data:** `HomeReserveFund`, `HomeReserveFundLineItem`, `HomeReserveFundContribution`, `HomeReserveFundRecalculation`.
- **Notes:** Reconciliation is real fuzzy-matching against logged `Expense` records — a suggestion requires the amount within ±25% and the date within ±3 months of the line item's expected window (`homeReserveFundReconciliation.service.ts`). Per an explicit "Phase 1" comment in that file, suggestions are computed live on every request rather than persisted, so there's no schema-backed "dismiss and don't show again" state yet — a declined suggestion can resurface until it's accepted or no longer eligible.

### Negotiation Shield
- **What it does:** Coaches a homeowner (or a buyer) through negotiating money-related disputes using their own documentation — contractor quotes that feel inflated, a home-insurance premium increase, an insurance claim settlement offer, or repair/credit requests during a home purchase after inspection — producing a structured negotiation case with an analysis and (for buyer cases) an outcome tracker.
- **User flow:** 1) User starts a case, choosing a scenario type (contractor quote review, insurance premium increase, insurance claim settlement, buyer inspection negotiation, or contractor urgency pressure) and a perspective (Homeowner or Buyer). 2) Provides manual input and/or uploads a supporting document, which gets parsed. 3) Runs the analysis. 4) For buyer-side repair/credit negotiations, tracks the seller's response and records the final outcome (accepted repair/credit, seller repaired, rejected, transferred to buyer).
- **Frontend:** `/dashboard/properties/[id]/tools/negotiation-shield/NegotiationShieldToolClient.tsx` (+`negotiationShieldApi.ts`).
- **Backend:** `negotiationShield.routes.ts` → cases CRUD, events, buyer-cases + buyer-outcome, manual input, document attach/parse, analyze (`controllers/negotiationShield.controller.ts` → `services/negotiationShield.service.ts`, plus scenario-specific services: `negotiationShieldDocumentParsing.service.ts`, `negotiationShieldContractorQuote.service.ts`, `negotiationShieldContractorUrgency.service.ts`, `negotiationShieldInsuranceClaimSettlement.service.ts`, `negotiationShieldInsurancePremium.service.ts`, `negotiationShieldBuyerInspection.service.ts`).
- **Data:** `NegotiationShieldCase`, `NegotiationShieldBuyerFinding`, `NegotiationShieldInput`, `NegotiationShieldDocument`, `NegotiationShieldAnalysis`, `NegotiationShieldDraft`.
- **Notes:** Confirmed rule/heuristic-based, not generative: no `GoogleGenAI`/Gemini import appears anywhere in `negotiationShield.service.ts`, the five scenario-analysis services, `negotiationShieldDocumentParsing.service.ts`, or `insuranceOcr.service.ts`. **Correction: `insuranceOcr.service.ts`'s claim above ("does not itself run text recognition") was wrong** — it was based on a static top-of-file import scan, which only shows `sharp` (used for image preprocessing). The file also runs a *dynamic* `await import('tesseract.js')` inside its extraction function (~line 56 — invisible to a static import grep, which is exactly how this got missed) and genuinely performs local OCR with Tesseract, not Gemini and not "no text recognition." Not generative either way — Tesseract OCR is deterministic text extraction, not an LLM call — but it is real text recognition. See [Home Health, Inventory & Maintenance](02-home-health-inventory-and-maintenance.md) for the corrected description of this same file. The case-orchestration and scenario engines evaluate parsed structured facts against fixed heuristics, not an LLM prompt.

### Service Quote Comparison
- **What it does:** Lets a homeowner collect multiple contractor/service-provider quotes for the same job into one workspace, normalize them for apples-to-apples comparison, resolve clarifying questions, and record which quote they selected and why — distinct from the insurance-quote handoff above.
- **User flow:** 1) Homeowner creates or opens a quote-comparison workspace for a property. 2) Adds quote proposals manually or by extracting one from an uploaded document. 3) Adds clarifications on ambiguous line items and resolves them. 4) Reviews the comparability view (normalized line items across quotes). 5) Selects a quote and records a decision transition; can link the decision back to other context (e.g., a project or job). 6) Optionally consents to later outcome measurement (did the work match the quote).
- **Frontend:** `/dashboard/properties/[id]/tools/quote-comparison/QuoteComparisonWorkspaceClient.tsx` (+`quoteDecisionUi.ts`).
- **Backend:** `quoteComparison.routes.ts` → workspace get-or-create/get, quotes CRUD (`POST`, `POST .../from-document`, `PATCH`, `DELETE`), disposition, clarifications (add/resolve), confirm, comparability, selection, decision transitions, context links, outcome-measurement consent (`controllers/quoteComparison.controller.ts` → `services/quoteComparison.service.ts`).
- **Data:** Quote workspace/proposal tables plus `ServiceQuoteDecisionTransition`, `ServiceQuoteDecisionContextLink`.
- **Notes:** This tool is scoped to **service/contractor quotes** for home work, not insurance quotes — don't confuse it with the Insurance Handoff flow above, which is a separate system with its own consent/governance model.

### Claims Assistance
- **What it does:** Helps a homeowner document, organize, and track an insurance claim end-to-end (documents, timeline, a type-specific checklist, and progress/SLA insights) — a homeowner-side claim tracker, not a live connection into an insurer's claims system.
- **User flow:** 1) Homeowner creates a claim for a property, choosing a claim type, which generates a type-specific checklist template. 2) Uploads supporting documents (single or bulk), including attaching documents directly to checklist items. 3) Logs timeline events and updates checklist item status as the claim progresses. 4) Views computed insights (days since last activity, SLA-based follow-up due dates, checklist completion). 5) Can export all claims for a property to CSV.
- **Frontend:** `/dashboard/properties/[id]/claims/ClaimsClient.tsx` (list) and `.../claims/[claimId]/ClaimDetailClient.tsx` (detail), using components in `components/claims/` (`ClaimStatusBadge`, `ClaimTimeline`, `ClaimChecklist`, `ClaimDocuments`, `ClaimProgressBar`, `ClaimBulkUploadModal`, `ClaimCreateModal`, `ClaimQuickActions`, `ClaimSubmitConfirmModal`).
- **Backend:** `claims.routes.ts` → list/create/get/update, documents (single + bulk), timeline, checklist item update + regenerate, insights, CSV export (`controllers/claims.controller.ts` → `services/claims/claims.service.ts`, checklist templates in `services/claims/claims.templates.ts`).
- **Data:** `Claim` (linked to `insurancePolicyId`/`warrantyId`), plus its documents, timeline events, and checklist items.
- **Notes:** Checklist generation is **template-based** (`CLAIM_CHECKLIST_TEMPLATES` keyed by claim type), not AI-generated, and "insights" (`getClaimInsights`) is a deterministic SLA calculation (days-since-status-change vs. per-status follow-up thresholds), not an LLM call — there is no external insurer claims-status integration. **Flagging as a product/implementation alignment issue, not a documentation gap:** a frontend nav/discovery audit found this feature absent from the capability/tool-discovery registry (`productFramework/capabilities/definitions/*.ts`) — it gets no `primaryJob` tag, no capability-suggestion surfacing, and no "Explore all tools" search visibility, despite being a real, fully-built feature.
