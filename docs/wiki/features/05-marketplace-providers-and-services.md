# Execution, Providers & Services

This page covers **Execute** — the stage of C2C's decision & action loop where a decision made elsewhere (Guidance, Coverage, a recall, a renovation case) actually gets done. Booking a provider is one execution path among several a homeowner can take — alongside doing nothing yet, monitoring, DIY, uploading information, or contacting a third party — not the product's identity; C2C's job is to get the homeowner to the right decision first, and this cluster is what happens after. That said, it's the one part of Contract to Cozy where the `PROVIDER` role is a first-class actor, not just a record homeowners browse, because execution-by-provider is a full two-sided marketplace in its own right: finding and booking a service pro, the whole booking lifecycle from request through review, provider registration/credential verification, price fairness checks, self-guided DIY projects, permit tracking, and the renovation/home-modification advisory pipeline. Wherever a flow has two sides, this page calls out what the `HOMEOWNER` sees versus what the `PROVIDER` sees in their own separate app surface.

### Provider Registration, Profile & Credentials
- **What it does:** Service pros sign up for their own account, build a business profile (service categories, radius, description, portfolio), and submit licenses/insurance/certifications for admin review. Homeowners see the outcome as a "Verified Pro" badge and a per-category eligibility signal before booking.
- **User flow:**
  1. Provider visits `/providers/join`, fills a two-step form (account info, then service category selection from an inspection/handyman taxonomy), and registers as `PROVIDER`.
  2. Provider logs in at `/providers/login` and lands on `/providers/dashboard`.
  3. Provider edits business profile at `/providers/profile` (business info, contact info, service categories, MFA settings via `MfaSettingsPanel`).
  4. Provider manages availability windows at `/providers/calendar` and portfolio photos at `/providers/portfolio`.
  5. Provider submits credentials (license/insurance/cert documents) at `/providers/credentials`; sees category eligibility and any compliance alerts (e.g. expiring credential).
  6. An admin reviews the queue and approves/rejects/revokes (separate admin surface, not part of this cluster).
  7. Homeowners browsing `/dashboard/providers` or a provider's profile page see a `VerifiedProBadge` computed from the public verification summary.
- **Frontend:** `app/providers/join/page.tsx`, `app/providers/login/page.tsx`, `app/providers/(dashboard)/layout.tsx` (provider shell nav/auth guard), `app/providers/(dashboard)/profile/page.tsx`, `app/providers/(dashboard)/calendar/page.tsx`, `app/providers/(dashboard)/portfolio/page.tsx`, `app/providers/(dashboard)/credentials/page.tsx`; shared chrome in `components/providers/ProviderShellTemplate.tsx` and `components/providers/ProviderAuthTemplate.tsx`; homeowner-facing trust UI in `components/features/providerTrust/VerifiedProBadge.tsx` and `components/features/providerTrust/CategoryEligibilityList.tsx` / `CredentialSubmitForm.tsx`.
- **Backend:** `GET/PATCH /api/providers/profile`, `GET/POST/PATCH/DELETE /api/providers/portfolio`, `GET/POST/PATCH/DELETE /api/providers/availability`, `GET /api/providers/search`, `GET /api/providers/:id`, `GET /api/providers/:id/services`, `GET /api/providers/:id/verification-summary`, `GET /api/providers/:id/reviews` — all in `provider.routes.ts` → `controllers/provider.controller.ts` → `services/provider.service.ts` / `services/provider-management.service.ts`. Credentials live in a separately-mounted route file, `providerCredential.routes.ts`: `GET /api/providers/me/credentials`, `POST /api/providers/me/credentials/documents`, `POST /api/providers/me/credentials`, `POST /api/providers/me/credentials/:id/renew`, `GET /api/providers/me/category-eligibility`, `GET /api/providers/me/compliance-alerts`, plus an MFA + capability-gated admin queue (`/api/admin/provider-credentials/*`) → `controllers/providerCredential.controller.ts` → `services/providerCredential.service.ts` / `services/providerCompliance.service.ts`.
- **Data:** `ProviderProfile`, `ProviderCredential`, `ProviderCredentialRequirement`, `ProviderCategoryEligibility`, `ProviderComplianceAlert`, `ProviderPortfolio`, `ProviderAvailability`, `ProviderServiceZone`, `Service`.
- **Notes:** `PROVIDER` and `ADMIN` roles can manage services/profile/portfolio/availability; `HOMEOWNER` only reads the public search/detail/verification-summary/reviews endpoints. `apps/backend/src/routes/provider.routes.ts.backup` exists alongside the live file and is stale cruft — it is not imported/mounted anywhere and should eventually be deleted. Similarly `apps/frontend/src/app/providers/(dashboard)/services/page.tsx.backup` sits next to the live services page. Booking creation still tolerates an unverified provider (see Booking Lifecycle notes below) — verification gates trust signaling, not the ability to book.

### Booking Lifecycle
- **What it does:** A homeowner requests a specific service from a specific provider for a property; the provider accepts/starts/completes it; the homeowner can leave a moderated review afterward that feeds the provider's public rating.
- **User flow:**
  1. Homeowner searches providers at `/dashboard/providers` (category + ZIP + radius filters, with "responsibility" prompts when the property's Home Record doesn't yet know who's responsible for that category of work, e.g. HOA vs owner).
  2. Homeowner opens a provider profile at `/dashboard/providers/[id]`, reviews services/reviews/portfolio, taps "Book now".
  3. Homeowner fills out `/dashboard/providers/[id]/book` — service, property, date/time window, description (min. 10 chars), special requests — and submits. A large set of optional context query params (guidance journey/step, inventory item, price-finalization id, radar handoff, etc.) let other tools deep-link into a pre-filled booking form.
  4. On the provider side, the request appears in `/providers/bookings` under the "Pending requests" tab; provider accepts (`confirm`) or declines (`cancel`).
  5. Provider marks the job `start`ed and later `complete`d (entering actual start/end time and final price) from the same queue or from `/providers/bookings/[id]`.
  6. Homeowner can edit (`PENDING`/`CONFIRMED`) or cancel (`PENDING`/`CONFIRMED`/`IN_PROGRESS`) from `/dashboard/bookings`, and views full detail + timeline at `/dashboard/bookings/[id]`.
  7. Once `COMPLETED`, the homeowner can leave a 1-5 star review with sub-ratings (quality/communication/value/professionalism); it is created `PENDING` and requires moderation before appearing publicly.
- **Frontend:** `app/(dashboard)/dashboard/providers/page.tsx` (search), `[id]/page.tsx` (profile), `[id]/book/page.tsx` (booking form); `app/(dashboard)/dashboard/bookings/page.tsx` (list, edit/cancel modals), `[id]/page.tsx` (detail + review form); provider side: `app/providers/(dashboard)/bookings/page.tsx` (tabbed pending/confirmed/history queue) and `app/providers/(dashboard)/bookings/[id]/page.tsx`; shared components `components/bookings/StatusBadge.tsx`, `components/bookings/BookingTimeline.tsx`, `components/providers/ProviderBookingQueueTemplate.tsx`, `components/features/booking/BookingEligibilityWarning.tsx`.
- **Backend:** `POST /api/bookings` (HOMEOWNER only), `GET /api/bookings`, `GET /api/bookings/:id`, `PUT /api/bookings/:id`, `POST /api/bookings/:id/confirm` (PROVIDER), `POST /api/bookings/:id/start` (PROVIDER), `POST /api/bookings/:id/complete` (PROVIDER), `POST /api/bookings/:id/cancel` (either side), `POST /api/bookings/:id/review` (HOMEOWNER, booking must be `COMPLETED`), `GET /api/bookings/:id/review` — `booking.routes.ts` → `controllers/booking.controller.ts` → `services/booking.service.ts` / `services/bookingEligibility.service.ts`.
- **Data:** `Booking`, `BookingTimeline`, `Service`, `Review` (moderation via `ReviewStatus`, `moderatedAt`/`moderatedBy`, optional link to `ProjectRecord` and `GuidanceJourney`).
- **Notes:** Role split is explicit and enforced at the route level (`requireRole`) as well as defense-in-depth in the controller/service. Booking creation returns a `providerEligibilityWarning` when the provider isn't yet credential-verified for the requested category — booking still succeeds, the homeowner just sees a warning toast. A 409 on create can mean either "you already have an active booking with this provider for this scope" (surfaces `existingBookingId`) or "execution blocked by incomplete guidance prerequisites" (surfaces guard details the UI renders as a `GuidanceWarningBanner`). Reviews only sync into a provider's aggregate rating once approved by moderation (per repo history, this pipeline was recently built out — see commit `d3a234e8`).

### Service Categories & Price Radar / Quote Comparison
- **What it does:** Backs the category taxonomy shown throughout the provider marketplace, and gives homeowners a fairness check on a quoted or actual price for a job ("is $450 for a water heater install reasonable here?").
- **User flow:**
  1. Category list (`ServiceCategoryIcon` + display names) populates the provider search filter and provider-onboarding category picker from `GET /api/service-categories`.
  2. For price fairness: from a property tool page, a homeowner enters a category, vendor name, and a quoted amount; the check is scored against benchmark data and returns a verdict (`UNDERPRICED` / `FAIR` / `HIGH` / `VERY_HIGH` / `INSUFFICIENT_DATA`).
  3. A separate, non-property-scoped Quote Comparison page (`/dashboard/quote-comparison`) lets a homeowner enter several vendor quotes side by side and runs each one through the same price-radar check, so they can compare vendors at a glance — this is the flow linked from provider search's "Compare multiple quotes first" and from the Resolution Center.
- **Frontend:** `components/ServiceCategoryIcon.tsx`; `app/(dashboard)/dashboard/quote-comparison/page.tsx` (multi-vendor quick comparison, built directly on the Service Price Radar API).
- **Backend:** `GET /api/service-categories` and `GET /api/service-categories/all` (`service-category.routes.ts`, reads `ServiceCategoryConfig` directly); `GET/POST /api/properties/:propertyId/service-price-radar/checks`, `GET .../checks/:checkId`, `POST .../events` (`servicePriceRadar.routes.ts` → `controllers/servicePriceRadar.controller.ts` → `services/servicePriceRadar.service.ts` + `servicePriceRadar.engine.ts`, benchmarked against `ServicePriceBenchmark`).
- **Data:** `ServiceCategoryConfig`, `ServiceRadarCheck`, `ServiceRadarCheckSystemLink`, `ServiceRadarUserAction`, `ServicePriceBenchmark` (+ its source/import-run/release/health tracking models).
- **Notes:** **Overlap worth knowing about:** there are two live quote-comparison surfaces. `/dashboard/quote-comparison` (covered here) is a lightweight multi-vendor fairness checker. A second, much larger implementation lives at `/dashboard/properties/[id]/tools/quote-comparison` (`QuoteComparisonWorkspaceClient.tsx`, ~1,500 lines) that is a full negotiation workspace tied into `PriceFinalization`/`PriceFinalizationTerm` and the guidance/fix flows — that one belongs more naturally to the Coverage/Risk/Financial Tools page (see Related pages) and is only mentioned here for completeness since it shares the same Service Price Radar backend. This project did not verify which surface is considered canonical going forward.

### DIY Project Tracker
- **What it does:** Lets a homeowner self-assess DIY skill level, browse a template library of do-it-yourself project guides, get an AI-generated step-by-step guide for a specific job, and track their own project (not a hired provider) from planning through completion or abandonment.
- **User flow:**
  1. Homeowner visits `/dashboard/diy`; if no skill profile exists yet, prompted to `/dashboard/diy/assess` (skill self-assessment feeding `getDiyDecision`, the "should I DIY this or hire a pro" decision engine).
  2. Browses featured/full template library at `/dashboard/diy/templates` and `/dashboard/diy/templates/[id]`.
  3. Starts a project from a template, or requests an AI-generated guide for a custom job; once the guide finishes generating (polled every 3s), a project can be created from it.
  4. Tracks progress at `/dashboard/diy/projects/[id]` — updating individual steps, then marking the project `complete` or `abandon`ed (or `HIRED_OUT` if they end up booking a provider instead).
- **Frontend:** `app/(dashboard)/dashboard/diy/page.tsx` (hub), `assess/page.tsx`, `templates/page.tsx`, `templates/[id]/page.tsx`, `projects/[id]/page.tsx`; components under `components/features/diy/` (`SkillProfileCard`, `TemplateCard`, `AiGuideSheet`, `DiyUtils`).
- **Backend:** all under `diy.routes.ts`: `GET/PUT /api/diy/skill-profile`, `GET /api/diy/templates(/featured|/:templateId)`, `POST /api/properties/:propertyId/diy/decision`, `POST/GET/PATCH /api/properties/:propertyId/diy/projects(...)`, `POST .../steps/:stepId`, `POST .../complete`, `POST .../abandon`, `POST/GET .../ai-guide` — → `controllers/diy.controller.ts` → `services/diy.service.ts`, `diyDecision.service.ts`, `diyAiGuide.service.ts`, `diyCompletion.service.ts` (plus an admin template-authoring surface gated by `CONTENT_AUTHOR` capability).
- **Data:** `DiySkillProfile`, `DiyProjectTemplate`, `DiyTemplateStep`, `DiyTemplateMaterial`, `DiyTemplateTool`, `DiyProject`, `DiyProjectStep`, `DiyProjectMaterial`, `DiyProjectTool`, `DiyAiGuide`.
- **Notes:** This is homeowner-only and entirely separate from the provider-facing marketplace and from the contractor Project Tracker described below — despite the name overlap, "DIY Project" (self-performed work) and "Project" (hired contractor execution, milestones/payments/change-orders) are two different systems on two different Prisma model families. Admin template status transitions were deliberately routed through a separate content-governance surface (`adminContentGovernance.routes.ts`) per `ADMIN_MODULE_FRD.md` — the direct admin PATCH-status route was removed from `diy.routes.ts`.

### Permit Tracker
- **What it does:** Tracks building permits for a property — pulled automatically from open municipal data sources where available, or entered manually — plus inspection milestones tied to each permit, AI-assisted "am I ready for this inspection" photo checks, detection of possible unpermitted work, and disclosure-export packaging for a sale.
- **User flow:**
  1. Homeowner opens `/dashboard/permits` for a selected property; sees active vs. historical permits and a summary.
  2. Can trigger an automated fetch from open data sources (polls fetch-job status every 4s) or add a permit manually at `/dashboard/permits/add`.
  3. Opens `/dashboard/permits/[id]` to see detail, record the "official" jurisdiction status, and manage inspection milestones for that permit.
  4. Before an inspection, can run an AI "readiness check" by uploading up to 12 photos of the work area — this is rate-limited (`expensiveAiRateLimiter`) since it's an AI call.
  5. Can review flagged possible unpermitted work at `/dashboard/permits/flags` (auto-detected via a scan, or added manually), and attach a remediation case.
  6. Can request a disclosure export (a package summarizing permit history, relevant for a home sale).
- **Frontend:** `app/(dashboard)/dashboard/permits/page.tsx` (hub), `add/page.tsx`, `[id]/page.tsx`, `flags/page.tsx`; components under `components/features/permits/` (`PermitCard`, `FetchStatusBanner`, `DisclosureExportButton`).
- **Backend:** `permitTracker.routes.ts` — fetch (`POST/GET .../permits/fetch(/status)`), CRUD (`GET/POST/PATCH/DELETE /api/properties/:propertyId/permits(/:permitId)`), official-status recording, inspection milestones (`.../permits/:permitId/inspections`), AI readiness checks (`.../inspections/:milestoneId/readiness-checks`, multer image upload + `inspectionReadiness.controller.ts`/`.service.ts`), unpermitted-work flags + detection scan (`.../permits/flags*`), disclosure export (`.../permits/disclosure*`), plus an `INTEGRATION_MANAGE`-gated admin surface for managing permit data sources → `controllers/permitTracker.controller.ts` → `services/permitTracker.service.ts`, `permitFetch.service.ts`, `permitDetection.service.ts`, `permitAdapters/`.
- **Data:** `PermitDataSource`, `PropertyPermitRecord`, `PermitInspectionMilestone`, `InspectionReadinessCheck`, `PermitUnpermittedFlag`, `PermitHistoricalFindingEvent`, `PermitFetchJob`, `PermitDisclosureExport`.
- **Notes:** Homeowner-only feature (no PROVIDER-side surface for permits in this codebase). SVG uploads are explicitly excluded from the readiness-check upload allowlist because an SVG can execute script when rendered as an `<img>`.

### Renovation & Home Modification Advisory
- **What it does:** A multi-stage pipeline for bigger renovation projects: explore AI-generated modification ideas against the homeowner's stated goals, convert a chosen idea into a formal renovation "case," determine what permits/licensing/HOA approval that case needs, track readiness, and (once ready) hand it off into project execution tracking.
- **User flow:**
  1. Homeowner opens `/dashboard/properties/[id]/renovations` — the case workspace — listing existing renovation cases plus any "retroactive candidates" (past timeline events the system thinks should have gone through this workflow but didn't, e.g. undocumented past work).
  2. To start something new, goes to `/dashboard/properties/[id]/renovations/explore`, which renders `HomeModificationAdvisor.tsx`: picks goals (accessibility, aging-in-place, resale value, energy efficiency, safety, etc.), optionally budget/timing, and gets back AI-generated modification options with cost ranges, benefits, and a "why this fits" rationale plus a confidence/evidence-source label.
  3. Can save/reject/select an option, then "create case" from a chosen option — this creates a `RenovationCase` from the `RenovationExploration`.
  4. Case detail pages walk through `/renovations/[caseId]/requirements` (what permits/licenses/HOA approval apply, generated then determined one by one), `/readiness` (a checklist of start-readiness items, with an owner-only override path for edge cases), and `/compliance` (HOA document review, compliance conditions/records).
  5. Once ready, an owner can hand the case off to project execution (`project-handoff`), which is where it becomes a trackable `ProjectRecord` with milestones/payments/change-orders (see Project Execution Tracker, next).
- **Frontend:** `app/(dashboard)/dashboard/properties/[id]/renovations/page.tsx` (case list + retroactive candidates), `explore/page.tsx` (→ `components/HomeModificationAdvisor.tsx`), `[caseId]/page.tsx`, `[caseId]/readiness/page.tsx`, `[caseId]/compliance/page.tsx`, `[caseId]/requirements/page.tsx`; shared helpers in `features/renovations/renovationWorkspace.ts`.
- **Backend:** all in `renovationCase.routes.ts` (mounted under `/api/properties/:propertyId/...`): explorations (`POST .../renovation-explorations`, `GET .../:explorationId`, `PATCH .../options/:optionId`, `POST .../options/:optionId/create-case`), cases (`GET/POST .../renovation-cases`, retroactive review, `GET .../:caseId`, `PATCH`, `transition`, `archive`), readiness (`GET/POST .../readiness*`, owner-only override), compliance workflow (HOA document reviews, compliance conditions/records), requirements + authority profiles, scope versions, participants, links, and an events/audit-trail endpoint — → `controllers/renovationCase.controller.ts` → `services/renovationCase.service.ts`, `renovationExplore.service.ts`, `renovationReadiness.service.ts`, `renovationRequirement.service.ts`, `renovationComplianceWorkflow.service.ts`, `renovationAuthorityIntegration.service.ts`, `renovationExecution.service.ts` (the `project-handoff` bridge). Household-role gating (`requireHouseholdRole('CONTRIBUTOR'|'OWNER')`) is layered on top of standard property auth for multi-person households.
- **Data:** `RenovationCase`, `RenovationExploration`, `RenovationUpgradeOption`, `RenovationStartReadinessItem`, `RenovationReadinessEvent`, `HoaApprovalDocumentReview`, `RenovationComplianceCondition`, `RenovationAuthorityProfile`, `RenovationRequirement`, `RenovationRequirementEvent`, `RenovationScopeVersion`, `RenovationCaseParticipant`, `RenovationCaseLink`, `RenovationCaseEvent`.
- **Notes — two superseded/legacy layers found:**
  - `homeModification.routes.ts` (`POST /api/modifications/recommend`) is a **deprecated shim**: it responds with a `Deprecation` header and a `Link: rel="successor-version"` pointing at the exploration endpoint, and internally just calls `createExploration()`. It exists only so old clients/links don't 404.
  - There is a **second, separate "Home Renovation Risk Advisor" backend** (`apps/backend/src/homeRenovationAdvisor/`, routes at `/api/home-renovation-advisor/...` — session create/update/evaluate/list/archive/export/compliance, plus a property-scoped `retroactive-candidates` and `batch-evaluate` endpoint) with its own model family (`HomeRenovationAdvisorSession`, `HomeRenovationPermitOutput`, `HomeRenovationTaxImpactOutput`, `HomeRenovationLicensingOutput`, `HomeRenovationAdvisorAssumption/DataPoint`, `HomeRenovationComplianceChecklist`). Its own dedicated frontend page (`HomeRenovationRiskAdvisorPageClient.tsx`, 558 lines) is **dead code** — confirmed by search, it is not imported anywhere; both `/dashboard/home-renovation-risk-advisor` and `/dashboard/properties/[id]/tools/home-renovation-risk-advisor` are now legacy routes that redirect straight into the Renovation Case workspace above. Of that entire session API surface, only `getRetroactiveCandidates()` is still called from live frontend code (from the renovations case list, to power "retroactive candidate" detection) — everything else (create/evaluate/batch-evaluate/archive/export/compliance session calls) has no live caller. This looks like a fully superseded feature that was replaced by the Renovation Case pipeline but never cleaned up; flagging as "planned/legacy, not confirmed live" rather than shipped.

### Project Execution Tracker (post-handoff)
- **What it does:** Once a renovation case (or a standalone project) is underway with a provider, this tracks milestones, payments, change orders, a progress log, issues, and a final closeout/completion package — the operational ledger for work actually happening.
- **Frontend:** Not directly explored in this pass (out of the routes list given for this cluster) — referenced here because `renovationExecution.service.ts` hands cases off into it, and because it exposes `getProviderExecutionReadiness` (a provider-facing readiness check before work starts).
- **Backend:** `projectTracker.routes.ts` — projects (`GET/POST/PATCH/DELETE /api/properties/:propertyId/projects*`), milestones, payments, change orders (including a compliance-review step), progress log, issues, and completion/closeout (`.../completion/checklist|confirm|package|share`, plus a public share-link endpoint that bypasses auth). Also exposes `GET .../projects/provider-readiness/:providerId` and `.../execution-alignment` (with a retry-reconciliation endpoint) — → `controllers/projectTracker.controller.ts` → `services/projectTracker.service.ts`.
- **Data:** `ProjectRecord`, `ProjectMilestone`, `ProjectPayment`, `ProjectChangeOrder`, `ProjectProgressLog`, `ProjectIssue`, `ProjectWriteBack`, `ProjectCloseoutShareLink`.
- **Notes:** This is where `PROVIDER` involvement in a renovation case becomes concrete and ongoing (readiness/alignment checks reference a specific `providerId`), distinct from the lighter one-off `Booking` lifecycle above. Full frontend verification of this surface was out of scope for this pass — noted here for completeness since it's the direct successor to a renovation-case handoff.

### Provider-Side Dashboard — Daily Workflow
- **What it does:** The complete separate app surface a `PROVIDER` account lives in day to day, distinct from anything a `HOMEOWNER` sees. Auth-gated: the shared layout redirects non-`PROVIDER` users to `/dashboard` and unauthenticated users to `/providers/login`.
- **User flow (a typical day):**
  1. Lands on `/providers/dashboard` — a command-center view: pending booking requests needing a response, today's jobs, recent bookings, completed-job revenue total, and quick links.
  2. Checks `/providers/bookings` — tabbed queue (Pending requests / Confirmed jobs / History) — accepts or declines pending requests, marks confirmed jobs as started/completed; a milestone celebration animation fires on completions.
  3. Manages `/providers/calendar` — blocked/available date windows (`ProviderAvailability`); recurring weekly working-hours UI exists in the form but is explicitly local-only/not yet persisted (no backend for it — see comment in the file referencing `PROVIDER_PORTFOLIO_AVAILABILITY_FRD.md`).
  4. Keeps `/providers/services` current — add/edit/delete the bookable services homeowners see on their profile (category, price, price unit, minimum charge, duration, active/inactive toggle).
  5. Uploads completed-work photos to `/providers/portfolio` (categorized, e.g. `PLUMBING`, `ROOFING`, `HVAC`...), reachable from a camera FAB on mobile.
  6. Maintains `/providers/credentials` — submits new credential documents, sees per-category eligibility and any compliance alerts (e.g., "insurance expiring soon").
  7. Edits `/providers/profile` — business info, contact info, service categories, and MFA settings.
- **Frontend:** `app/providers/(dashboard)/layout.tsx` (nav shell, bottom tab bar on mobile, camera FAB), `dashboard/page.tsx`, `bookings/page.tsx` + `bookings/[id]/page.tsx`, `calendar/page.tsx`, `services/page.tsx` (note: a stale `services/page.tsx.backup` sits alongside it), `portfolio/page.tsx`, `credentials/page.tsx`, `profile/page.tsx`; shared chrome `components/providers/ProviderShellTemplate.tsx` and `components/providers/ProviderBookingQueueTemplate.tsx`.
- **Backend:** Reuses the same `booking.routes.ts` and `provider.routes.ts` self-service endpoints documented above (`/services`, `/profile`, `/portfolio`, `/availability`, plus booking `confirm`/`start`/`complete`), and `providerCredential.routes.ts`'s `/providers/me/*` endpoints.
- **Notes:** This entire surface is `PROVIDER`-role-only (with `ADMIN` also permitted on the self-service management endpoints per the route middleware, presumably for support/impersonation tooling — not explored further here).
