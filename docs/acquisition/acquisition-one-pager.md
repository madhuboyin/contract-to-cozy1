## ContractToCozy
**Homeowner Intelligence Platform and Strategic Technology Asset**

ContractToCozy is a pilot-stage platform built around a simple idea: if one system understands the homeowner, the property, its assets, documents, risks, finances, local context, and current goals, it can surface more useful next actions across the full homeownership journey.

### The Problem
Homeowners manage one of their largest financial and physical assets through disconnected systems. Mortgage details live in one place, insurance in another, maintenance history somewhere else, warranties in email, permits in local records, and useful programs or savings opportunities often go unnoticed entirely.

That fragmentation creates missed decisions, not just inconvenience. The founder’s own experience included missing New Jersey ANCHOR homeowner benefits for two years, missing a favorable refinance window, and paying more than necessary for homeowners insurance. Those are origin examples, not measured platform outcomes, but they reflect the core product insight: homeowners rarely need one isolated tool. They need a connected understanding of what matters now and what to do next.

Most products address a narrow job to be done. ContractToCozy was built as a wider homeowner intelligence system designed to connect signals across ownership, protection, maintenance, savings, improvement, documents, and future planning.

### What Has Been Built
Repository review found a substantial body of working software and product architecture, including 36 dedicated property-scoped tool routes plus additional core workflows for onboarding, inventory, maintenance, claims, incidents, and seller preparation.

**1. Home Profile, Assets, and Operating Context**

The platform includes structured property onboarding, persistent property and homeowner models, room and inventory management, document linking, item-level coverage analysis, and a status board that organizes assets by condition, age, warranty state, maintenance burden, and recommended action.

**2. Protection, Maintenance, and Risk Intelligence**

ContractToCozy includes working implementations for maintenance tasks, seasonal workflows, incidents, claims, inspection workflows, coverage analysis, risk replay, home-event monitoring, permit tracking, and guidance journeys. Rather than treat these as isolated modules, the architecture is designed so maintenance state, claims context, coverage gaps, and property events can influence one another.

**3. Financial Opportunity and Cost Intelligence**

The codebase includes refinance monitoring with scenario calculations and rate-ingestion jobs, home savings comparisons across recurring household categories, reserve fund and capital timeline workflows, true-cost and cost-growth views, property-tax workflows, negotiation support, and service-price benchmarking. The value is not a single calculator; it is the ability to connect home finances to property condition, insurance posture, timing, and upcoming decisions.

**4. Improvement, Renovation, and Sale-Readiness Workflows**

There is implemented work across DIY project planning, renovation compliance analysis, permit milestones, inspection-readiness photo review, project tracking, seller prep, financing scenarios, and a home digital twin with saved what-if scenarios. Together they demonstrate a broad product thesis around improving, preparing, and repositioning the home over time.

**5. Documents, Neighborhood, and Ownership Memory**

The platform also includes a homeowner vault, home digital will, household-sharing workflows, neighborhood change radar, a Home Gazette layer, and material-spec tracking.

### Why It Is Strategically Different
The differentiation is not the existence of many tools. It is the shared model behind them.

ContractToCozy includes working implementations of a property-centered domain model, guidance signals and journeys, inventory and asset records, homeowner financial context, event timelines, scenario storage, and recommendation logic. That creates the foundation for a question many incumbents still do not answer well: given what the system knows about this homeowner and this home, what is the next useful action?

Today, that shows up in concrete ways such as coverage analysis tied to insurance, warranties, claims, and inventory; refinance monitoring tied to mortgage snapshots and market-rate ingestion; asset and condition data feeding status workflows and digital-twin scenarios; and neighborhood or property events feeding monitoring and editorial surfaces.

### Strategic Buyer Value
A strategic buyer would not be acquiring traction. It would be acquiring roadmap acceleration.

The repository contains immediate access to a broad homeowner product architecture, implemented workflows across multiple categories, a reusable domain and data model, UI and UX patterns for homeowner decision support, APIs and background jobs, scoring and recommendation logic, and infrastructure assets. For a buyer that already has distribution, users, capital, data, or integration leverage, this could reduce the need to start from a blank page and make it easier to selectively absorb the most relevant modules into an existing platform.

The codebase also reflects a wide map of homeowner pain points and product responses across savings, protection, maintenance, planning, and ownership lifecycle moments. Rebuilding that problem map internally would likely require repeating meaningful discovery and prototyping effort.

### Current Stage and Transaction Intent
ContractToCozy is transparently an early-stage technology asset. It is in pilot stage, has no material production user base, no validated recurring revenue model, and should not be described as production-proven. Its current self-hosted Raspberry Pi Kubernetes setup reflects low-cost experimentation rather than the core acquisition value.

A buyer would still need security review, integration planning, production hardening, and cloud deployment decisions. Those are normal next steps for an early-stage strategic technology transfer.

ContractToCozy may be relevant to organizations already serving homeowners that want to accelerate expansion into a more connected homeowner intelligence experience. A confidential discussion and product demonstration can quickly determine overlap with a buyer’s current or future roadmap.
